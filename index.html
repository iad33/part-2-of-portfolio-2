Composing Digital Media Portfolio

Introduction to New Media


COMP 1

Comp 1 consisted of a get to know me assignment and an introduction to coding.

COMP 2

Comp 2 involved creating a glitch website using HTML and CSS.

COMP 3

Comp 3 involved creating a more complex website with multiple pages and advanced CSS features.

Course Goals


1. Consider technology’s role in acts of making

Technology is a key element of the work being done, not merely a means to do it. With the Dear Data project, for instance, my iPhone screen time statistics serve as source material in that I create a bar graph based on my app usage. The glitch project involves using CSS to glitch the visual language of a photo. Here, I am forced to consider not only what effect the glitch produces but also what it says about the vulnerability of digital experiences. Finally, the webtext assignments make clear the role that the available technology plays in shaping what one can actually do as a maker. The Pitt Fuego template, blocks of hero, parallax, and photo grid determine the stories I can tell.

2. Embrace experimentation

The element of experimentation was seen in the amount of deviation I took away from my initial ideas. In the project, "Dear Data," I took away the intricate nature of the graph and drew it using ruler lines so that it would be possible to hand-draw it. While the glitch effect went from a disorganized and heavily distorted one to the restrained one using channel drift because the initial one seemed like noise rather than deliberate effort. My webtext projects were the most experimental in their concepts; while I initially thought of doing the "cliché glossary that eats itself," I later did the cat one, and I tried out different narrative structures in "Escape the Cave."

3.Engage with others in a workshop environment

Even though I was mostly working independently at this point, the workshop approach was very helpful every monday getting to talk issues out with my peers. Each glitch effect went through several iterations, scaling back the first one because it appeared to be too much. The Escape the Cave sitemap was deliberately designed for feedback before coding even started, the fundamental workshop approach of putting something together so that there is a chance to see how others react to it.

4. Practice revision within and across media forms

Each project evolved through multiple media stages to reach its final incarnation. The process of creating Dear Data began with raw data, followed by a visualization, then description, and edits were made along the way with each transition, editing the text down further and making the image simpler to hand-draw. This is most obvious in Escape the Cave; the project started as text (Plato), became a pitch, and then became a sketched-out sitemap, then a flowchart visualization, and finally the actual body text and button labels. Again, this was revision happening in transition between media, rather than polishing an existing piece of writing.

5. Learn to present your work

We were required to present our website “All About Me” and the Dear Data project for one to two minutes to the whole class. It should be noted that I was informed about it in class by the professor; thus, I could not prepare for it beforehand. It means that I had to come up with an explanation of the website's pages on the spot without any preparation at all instead of coming with a pre-prepared speech. This also meant the need to determine which aspects of my website should be explained verbally (why I had chosen to do things in such a way) and which could be left for the visuals as I did not have enough time to overthink anything. The fact is that presenting projects in this way, spontaneously, is very useful for the future; it is obvious that most presentations will not be prepared for in advance and will be spontaneous.

Sound and Image


Comp 4

Comp 4 involved creating a wedding soundscape by layering and editing recorded audio in audacity.

Comp 5

Comp 5 involved producing an exploratory podcast on athletes playing through pain and mental health issues.

Comp 6

Comp 6 involved creating a collage of orchard and pumpkin patch photos with friends, paired with passages from Barthes’s Camera Lucida.

Comp 7

Comp 7 involved creating a photo essay of Pittsburgh pictures from my weekend, sequenced in Premiere Pro as a dérive.

Course Goals

1. Consider technology’s role in 3cts of making

In the case of Sound Composition, technology became not only the means of producing the work. In Sound Composition, using the software Audacity, I composed a whole atmosphere out of different recordings by layering, cutting, and adjusting sound levels. It can be said that technology determined the entire process of creating this composition. As I could isolate, loop, and change sound levels, I should have chosen what part of the wedding would be heard: the party itself, the crowd, the music, or the silences in between. My inspiration for this work came from the article written by Solomos on the topic of the nature of sound space, sound environment, and ambiance. According to him, the recording is not a neutral representation of the event. The reason is the fact that it is the construction of a sound environment which is achieved through the selection of sounds. The resource Digital Audio Explained made me look even deeper into the matter of sound editing and helped me to understand that digitization turns the sound into data, which makes some manipulation possible.


2. Embrace experimentation

The podcasting assignment was based on Montaigne’s “Of Cannibals” and other exploratory examples like Serial and Freakonomics, which follow a question without having an argument prepared at first. This allowed me to take the topic and explore it instead of having an answer ready to solve the issue. Also, I used my experience in basketball and working with Division I athletes to make the topic more realistic for me personally. The experiment showed me that an important topic could be talked about in an exploratory way and that in audio, pacing and pauses have as much value as words do in writing.


3.Engage with others in a workshop environment

The most helpful workshop comments were about my pumpkin patch collage. The juxtaposition of pictures of my friends and Barthes’s concepts from Camera Lucida was a personal decision, and I could not see objectively if the connections were evident. Their explanations of how they interpreted the images gave me an insight into the difference between my interpretation of a photo and its interpretation by someone who has never seen it. This corresponds to Barthes’s explanation of what the difference is between what a picture depicts and what it provokes. Offering feedback on the collages of my classmates helped me be a better observer, as I had to find a way to explain clearly why the connection between the quote and the picture was or was not appropriate.


4. Practice revision within and across media forms

My Pittsburgh photo essay went through many transformations before taking shape in its final form. At first it was the exploration of the city through a weekend of wandering, then it was a compilation of pictures, and finally it became a sequenced and timed composition using Premiere Pro software. The 5-minute write based on the dérive served as the initial stage of translation that helped me to transform my experience into something to be incorporated in the edit, and De Bord’s “Theory of the Dérive” provided me with a way to perceive my weekend as uncontrolled wandering around the city. Every transition between mediums entailed the process of cutting and rewriting. The selection of photographs, the time each picture spent in focus, and the sequence itself brought certain meaning to the weekend.


5. Learn to present your work

My Sound Composition piece for the Wedding was shown to the class. It is different from presenting a website or photos since the audience won’t be able to look at them after they heard them once in order. So, I had to decide which elements I wanted to explain before playing my work and which I wanted to leave for the listeners to experience for themselves. I chose to talk about my decisions, but not explain what they can hear for themselves. The process of presenting helped me understand which moments were clear to the audience without my explanation. The fact that I listened to it in the room full of people made me pay attention to things that I did not pay attention to after editing my piece.
